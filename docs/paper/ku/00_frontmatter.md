## Abstract

The persistent fragmentation of human knowledge across languages, geographies, and temporal boundaries remains a fundamental impediment to the construction of globally coherent knowledge systems. Existing representation formalisms—ranging from RDF triples to large-scale embedding models—fail to reconcile the competing demands of compactness, semantic fidelity, decentralized operation, and cross-lingual accessibility within a single unified framework. This paper introduces the *Knowledge Unit* (KU), a bio-inspired knowledge representation that draws structural analogies from molecular biology to achieve a self-contained, content-addressed, and language-agnostic encoding of atomic knowledge. The KU architecture is organized as a three-layer model: (1) *CoreDna*, a compact binary instruction sequence that encodes semantic content through an opcode-based instruction set; (2) *Epigenetics*, a runtime metadata layer that governs trust propagation, inter-KU bonding, and metabolic lifecycle signals without altering the underlying CoreDna; and (3) *Expression*, a natural language rendering layer that generates human-readable output on demand from the CoreDna encoding, analogous to the transcription and translation of genetic information into functional proteins.

The CoreDna layer defines 32 opcodes (0x00–0x1F), each encoded within an 8-bit instruction byte comprising a 5-bit opcode field and a 3-bit modifier. Semantic content is structured through 13 gene types—Fact, Comparison, Process, Causal, Taxonomy, Temporal, and Spatial (types 0–6, encoded directly in the instruction byte), and Conditional, Analogy, Negation, Quantitative, Relational, and Definition (types 7–12, accessed via the EXTENDED opcode mechanism). Numeric literals are represented through 7 NumericValue types distinguished by sentinel bytes: F64 (0xF9), U8 (0xFA), U16 (0xFB), I16 (0xFC), U32 (0xFD), I32 (0xFE), and F32 (0xFF). Concept identifiers employ a 5-tier variable-length integer encoding: Tier 0 maps 1 byte to 128 values, Tier 1 maps 2 bytes to approximately 16K values, Tier 2 maps 3 bytes to approximately 2M values, Tier 3 maps 4 bytes to approximately 268M values, and Tier 4 maps 5 bytes to approximately 34.6B values, with Tiers 5–7 reserved for future expansion. Tier 0 accommodates 80 universal concepts organized into 8 semantic groups, of which 74 are defined, 47 are reserved for domain extension, and 1 serves as a sentinel. Each concept is globally identified by a Content-addressed Concept Identifier (CCID), computed as a 128-bit truncated BLAKE3 hash, ensuring collision-resistant identity without centralized coordination. The Concept Table provides a self-contained mapping from wire-local identifiers to CCIDs directly within the wire format. At the ecosystem level, a ConceptRegistry of approximately 200 MB indexes approximately 8 million concepts with O(1) lookup, achieving an estimated 99.9% coverage of general-domain knowledge. The wire format follows the structure: MAGIC (0x4B) | VER_META (1 byte) | [CONCEPT_TABLE] | INSTRUCTIONS | END (0x1E) | CRC-16 (2 bytes), where the VER_META byte encodes bits [7:5] as the version field (currently 2), bits [4:1] as the gene type, and bit [0] as a flag indicating the presence of a Concept Table. Each KU is globally identified by a Content Identifier (CID), defined as the 32-byte BLAKE3 hash of the encoded CoreDna bytes. The system supports decentralized state convergence through 5 conflict-free replicated data types (CRDTs): GCounter, PNCounter, LWWRegister, ORSet, and VectorClock. The Epigenetics layer incorporates a TrustSection comprising 6 Proof-of-Meaningful-Verification (PoMV) signals, a vocabulary of 33 bond types governing inter-KU relationships, and 11 epistemic status levels that qualify the certainty and provenance of knowledge claims. A 3-tier encoding pipeline transforms structured semantic input through successive stages of concept resolution, instruction emission, and wire-format serialization.

The reference implementation is written in Rust and comprises over 15,000 lines of code across more than 40 modules, validated by a test suite of 827 tests spanning unit, integration, and property-based testing. Wire-size benchmarks demonstrate substantial compression relative to equivalent UTF-8 natural language text: a factual description of the breaststroke swimming technique (323 bytes in UTF-8) encodes to 88 bytes across 3 KUs, yielding a compression ratio of 3.7×; a multi-faceted description of rocket propulsion (1,078 bytes in UTF-8) encodes to 172 bytes across 5 KUs, yielding a compression ratio of 6.3×. The principal novel contributions of this work are: (1) the three-layer bio-inspired architecture separating encoding, metadata, and expression; (2) a compact 32-opcode binary instruction set for semantic encoding; (3) the CCID content-addressed concept identity mechanism; (4) a 5-tier variable-length integer encoding with universal concept embedding; (5) CRDT-based decentralized state convergence; (6) the Epigenetics layer integrating trust, bonding, and epistemic qualification; and (7) the ConceptRegistry as a scalable, offline-capable concept resolution infrastructure.
